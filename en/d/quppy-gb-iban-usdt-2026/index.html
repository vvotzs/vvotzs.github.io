Highlight

Open a GB-prefixed UK personal IBAN online with a Chinese ID card or passport, send and receive euros over SEPA and sterling over Faster Payments with no Quppy transfer fee, and use a custodial wallet that sends and receives USDT; the Quppy card is not live yet and only a waitlist is open

What you get

QUPPY EUROPE OÜ (Estonian registration 14418677) builds only the technology and app and states it is not a bank; fiat accounts come from payment providers, with 2PAYAPP LIMITED named in current terms for euro SEPA, and EEA crypto custody and exchange from the Malta MiCA-licensed Payhound Limited. The personal multi-currency IBAN holds EUR and GBP in one account, support quoting GB12CLJU98523412345678 (GB plus BIC CLJU, sort code 985234 and account 12345678), with euros over SEPA Standard or Instant and sterling over Faster Payments or CHAPS. Incoming GBP is unavailable, as are card top-ups and withdrawals; USD, local European IBANs and Solana are coming soon and the Quppy card is listed as such. Supported EUR and GBP transfers cost €0 or £0 with free IBAN opening, while exchange goes at the app's quote and an older fee page lists 1.12% on high-risk transactions, 2.1% between EUR and GBP, a 50 compliance fee for late KYC documents and 5 a month after 183 idle days. The custodial wallet holds BTC, ETH, TRON, USDC and USDT, with USDT on ERC-20 and TRC-20 excluded for EEA users but open elsewhere. The availability directory shows China (personal) with multi-currency IBAN, crypto wallet, exchange and local payment methods available and the card coming soon, while the UK shows all five unavailable; the card is only an email waitlist, which the site says is not an application.

Conditions

18 or over with in-app KYC: passport or ID in Latin letters front and back plus a selfie, with community tests passing Chinese IDs and mainland numbers and a self-entered address; do not put the UK as residence or nationality; the app is missing from some App Stores (China and the US) with an Android APK on the site; incoming GBP, card top-ups and card withdrawals are unavailable; the Quppy card is not live; passing KYC does not guarantee an account.

Steps

  1. Download the app from quppy.com (iOS needs an Apple ID from another region, Android can use the APK from the official site), register with an email and set a four-digit PIN
  2. In Verification, enter name, gender and phone number, choose an ID card or passport, photograph both sides and take a selfie holding it, and enter your real address
  3. After approval open the IBAN account, tap Receive, and note the GB-prefixed IBAN with its six-digit sort code and eight-digit account number
  4. Fund it from a bank account in your own name in EUR or GBP, or create a USDT wallet (TRC-20 or ERC-20), withdraw USDT from an exchange and exchange it to EUR or GBP in the app
  5. For the card, leave your email on the waitlist at quppy.com/en/personal/card and wait for the official launch in your market

Watch out

Quppy is not a bank; the IBAN is provided by a payment partner and is not covered by deposit insurance. The company is Estonian, EEA crypto services run through Payhound and non-EEA crypto through an unnamed local licensed provider, so the route to remedy a problem is long. Published fee figures disagree (Quppy publishes no exchange fee, a legacy support page says 2.1%, users quote 1.5%; for the dormant fee both 5 and 100 EUR/GBP circulate), so the in-app quote and terms govern. The dormant-account fee starts 183 days after the last transaction and keeps charging monthly. Crypto funding carries blockchain and compliance risk, large amounts can trigger source-of-funds questions, and this is not a place to park savings. Using the GB IBAN to activate Wise is a user claim that Wise has never promised to accept. The card is not launched, so any claim that it can be opened now is false or third-party. The app is not in the mainland China App Store and switching store regions carries its own risk; when verifying with an ID card, a polyphonic surname is romanised from the document and can end up differently from what you expect, in which case a passport is the alternative.
