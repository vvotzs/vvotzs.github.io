Highlight

From 18 Nov 2026, households with a 5–18 year old signing up or porting to Y!mobile Simple 3 M/L get ¥1,000 (¥1,100 with tax) off the monthly fee for 12 months, stackable with the PayPay Card discount (including a ¥200 boost) and the Hikari set discount, taking Simple 3 M (30GB) as low as ¥858 a month

What you get

SoftBank's official press release (issued 16 Nov 2026, starting 18 Nov) and Mynavi's November round-up confirm: the parent-child discount targets users aged 5–18 and cuts ¥1,000 (¥1,100 with tax) a month for a year on Simple 3 M/L from the month after sign-up. From the same date the PayPay Card discount boost raises the PayPay Card discount from ¥300 to ¥500 a month (¥550 with tax) for those users and their families. Combined with the Hikari set discount (A), Simple 3 M comes to ¥780 (¥858 with tax) a month for the first year, returning to ¥1,958 from month 14. The separate SIM-only switching campaign pays up to ¥15,000 in PayPay points, but its stacking and exclusion rules differ, so check the application page.

Conditions

Japanese residency and ID verification; the user must be 5–18 years old (family members share the boost); new sign-up or MNP to Simple 3 M/L; the 12-month discount starts the month after applying and ends from month 14; the PayPay Card discount requires paying with a PayPay Card and completing PayPay KYC plus Yahoo! JAPAN ID linking; ports from SoftBank, LINEMO and LINE Mobile are excluded from the SIM cashback.

Steps

  1. Prepare ID for the 5–18 year old user and a payment method
  2. Order Simple 3 M/L as a new line or MNP on Y!mobile's official online store or Yahoo! store
  3. Link the PayPay Card and Yahoo! JAPAN ID as prompted
  4. After activation confirm the bill drops by ¥1,100 with tax from the next month
  5. Apply separately for the Hikari set discount or SIM cashback if you want them stacked

Watch out

The discount lasts only 12 months and the price returns to normal from month 14, so it is poor value long term; the SIM cashback conflicts with some older campaigns and excludes past Y!mobile online-store PayPay recipients; a PayPay Card is a credit card, so decide whether it is worth opening one for this.
