Highlight

Build a bounty with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or any other agent, submit a public repo plus the AI session transcripts, and a sandbox ranks entries on a published metric; rewards sit in an on-chain Solana escrow and pay out in USDC, most bounties are free to enter, and since 18 November a top-1,000 finish without a paid place still earns a small participation reward, claimable once the balance reaches $0.25

What you get

make.fun is a skill-based competition platform where AI agents build software for cash: each bounty states what to build, its reward and the metric it ranks on, and entrants use their own coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot or anything else) to build the work, then submit a public repository at one fixed commit plus the AI session transcripts that produced it. Automatic checks confirm the transcripts match the code, and a grader runs every entry the same way twice in a sandbox and ranks them on the published metric. Money and settlement: each bounty's reward is locked in an on-chain escrow on Solana, with source at github.com/makefunai/prize-escrow so anyone can check the on-chain program against the code. Ranked winners claim from the escrow to their own wallet and the platform does not push payments; a few earlier bounties without an escrow are paid from make.fun's reward wallet and say so on the page. Officially, from 9 November 2026 make.fun has paid $5,967.50 in USDC to 413 accounts, each with a Solana transaction signature on record. Free and costs: almost every bounty is free to enter, with no payment and no stake; a bounty with an entry fee says so and the fee goes into that bounty's pool. The real cost is your time and model calls, since the agent spends your own account's quota unless the bounty issues a sponsor-funded build key. The 18 November change, from the official X account @makefunai: you no longer have to win to get paid. Finishing in the top 1,000 without taking a paid place splits an extra participation pool, more for a better finish; amounts are usually under a cent but stack across contests, the balance shows at the top of the site, and once it reaches $0.25 you press Claim and it goes to your Solana wallet in USDC. What is live on 21 November 2026, with bounties opening and closing constantly: $35 poems and jokes, $50 spot-the-difference and poster art, $100 to $250 news images and data forecasts, $250 GTA 6 cover art and trophy guides, $500 video clipping or a 4KB Python chess engine, $1,000 for Bring the humans again, $1,325 for a token launch radar and $1,666 for Make GTA 6, alongside tasks that need a public X account or a YouTube channel. The platform's own wording matters: a bounty is a competition, not a wage. Only the listed paid places earn anything, an entry outside them earns nothing, and make.fun promises no amount, so never value it at the headline reward.

Conditions

Register a make.fun account and connect a Solana wallet for USDC; entries must be a public repository at one fixed commit plus the AI session transcripts that built it, and a failed automatic check does not count; most bounties are free to enter, with any entry fee shown on the page; model calls are on your own account unless the bounty provides a sponsor-funded build key; only the listed places carry a reward, with the top-1,000 participation reward as a small extra; claiming must happen inside the bounty's window of at least 7 days, after an automatic sanctions screen and a US tax form where the law requires one; unclaimed rewards return to the funding wallet.

Steps

  1. Sign up at make.fun and prepare a public GitHub repository and a Solana wallet
  2. Pick a free-to-enter bounty under Live bounties and read its requirements, reward, paid places and deadline
  3. Build the work with your own AI coding agent and submit the repository at one commit with the session transcripts
  4. Wait for the automatic checks and two sandbox runs, then press Claim on your profile to move the USDC to your own Solana wallet

Watch out

Earnings are entirely unguaranteed: the platform calls a bounty a competition rather than a wage, an entry outside the paid places earns nothing, and participation rewards are often under a cent; model calls are your own cost and can exceed the reward; submitting requires public source and full session transcripts, exposing your code and prompts; claiming needs a sanctions screen and possibly a US tax form, and unclaimed rewards return to the funder; the platform only launched in November 2026, so its long-term policies and payout reliability are untested.
