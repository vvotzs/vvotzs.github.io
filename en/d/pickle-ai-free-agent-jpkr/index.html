What you get

Pickle is a personal AI agent that actually does the work rather than only chatting. Two things define it. First, a cloud browser: the official help says Pickle uses a real web browser to work sites the way a person would — booking restaurants and flights, buying things, cancelling subscriptions, reading bank and brokerage records, requesting documents on government sites. That browser lives in the cloud, separate from your phone, and if a step only you can do comes up (a CAPTCHA, or you'd rather type your own password) the screen is handed to you to finish. Second, phone calls: for places with no booking page — a restaurant, a clinic's front desk, your landlord, a customer service line — Pickle calls for you, the call can be followed live, the result is reported back, and the record sits in Other chats. Before a payment or booking it shows a card of what it will do and at what price and waits for your go-ahead; if the amount rises right before paying, it asks again. Free plan (per the Japanese help centre): in Japan every account is free with no paid plan, replaced by a daily usage limit that resets to 0% at 04:00 Japan time. Replies, calls from Chat, handed-off tasks and scheduled tasks all count; conversations on 3D World calls and Pickle's own fixed routines do not. At the limit the input box turns off and scheduled tasks are skipped until 04:00. Reset tickets: a Full reset ticket returns the day's usage to 0%; a Half reset ticket lowers it by 50 points (never below 0%). You get one Full ticket for finishing onboarding, one each for you and each friend who completes sign-up through your invite link, and you can buy more in the app at prices shown on the purchase screen (the US App Store lists $3.99 for a Full ticket and $1.99 for a Half ticket; Japan about ¥600 each — it varies by store). Tickets don't expire and only restore the current day's usage; they don't raise the daily cap.

Conditions

Applies automatically to Japan/Korea accounts, with no card or subscription needed. A ticket only restores usage already spent that day and does not raise the daily cap; unused tickets stay but the day's usage resets anyway at 04:00. Once the limit is reached, new tasks can't start and scheduled tasks are skipped. The app runs on iOS and Android only (no web version).

Steps

  1. Install Pickle from the App Store or Google Play and finish sign-up (Japan/Korea accounts are free, no card)
  2. Say what you need in Chat, e.g. "Book a table for two at 7 PM Friday and ask if they have parking"
  3. Pickle works the site in its cloud browser; where a site can't help, it calls and the call shows in Other chats
  4. Check the task card for what it will do and what it will cost, then let it run (it always checks before paying or booking)
  5. Out of usage: My Pickle → Daily usage limit → use a reset ticket, or continue right away from the "Continue using" card
  6. Want more tickets: invite friends — you and each friend who completes sign-up get one Full reset ticket

Watch out

Terms differ sharply by region: the Japanese and Korean help pages say all accounts are free with no paid plan, while the English (US) pages still require a subscription and list Plus/Pro/Max/Ultra, so check the card in your own app. A ticket only rescues the current day and does not raise the base limit. Pickle has not published the exact daily credit allowance, how many points each action costs, or ticket prices (prices show on the purchase screen) — those are marked unverified. Pickle can make mistakes: check the task card and the result before paying or booking, and never type passwords or card numbers into the chat.
