Highlight

Sesame's Maya, Miles and other characters are known for unusually lifelike real-time voices: the free plan gives 30 minutes of calls a day plus limited weekly credits, straight from iOS or Android. Sesame Link also lets one phone call walk you through your Claude Code and Codex sessions — hear each one's status, focus on a session and talk to it live, approve permissions, start new work.

What you get

Sesame (Sesame AI, Inc., from the co-founders of Oculus) builds voice-first personal AI. Its site lists four characters — Maya, Miles, Simone and Charlie — you call or text in the iOS and Android app, and when signed in each remembers its past conversations with you. The free plan is $0: 30 minutes of talk time a day (resets daily) and limited weekly credits for texts, tasks and higher intelligence (resets weekly), with the same features on every plan. When the allowance runs out you can keep texting, but intelligence drops and some capabilities are restricted. Plus is $20/mo for 2 hours a day (4x) and 4x the weekly credits; Pro is $100/mo for 10 hours a day (20x); add-ons are 1 hour of talk time for $5 or 5 hours for $20, and 1,000 credits for $5 or 5,000 for $20 (one-time purchases never expire and are used after the plan allowance). Plus has a free 7-day trial, and subscriptions are managed through the App Store or Google Play. Sesame Link (link.sesame.com) puts the coding agents in your terminal on your phone: install a CLI on the computer running Claude Code, Codex, Pi, Cursor, Hermes or OpenCode and sign in with the same Sesame account, then use the browser web client or simply call your Sesame character to ask "What are my sessions doing?" and hear each session's status, say "Focus on …" to talk to Claude or Codex live, approve or deny permission requests by voice, and start or end sessions. The code, tools and accounts stay on your own computer; Link only relays through Sesame's cloud.

Conditions

No card is needed for the free plan. Talk time resets daily and credits weekly; calls use talk time and anything the agent goes and does uses credits, while Max intelligence uses both. When the allowance is gone you can still text, with lower intelligence and some restrictions. Sesame Link needs a Sesame account plus the CLI installed on a computer with the coding agent installed and signed in (Claude Code also needs tmux 3.4+; the desktop apps don't count), and runs on macOS and x86-64 Linux (preview) only. Claude Code is fully supported; Codex, Pi and Cursor are previews; Hermes and OpenCode are experimental. Where the app isn't listed you can only use the web Research Preview, which does less than the app. Sesame officially supports English, with early, less reliable support for Chinese, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Japanese and Korean.

Steps

  1. Install "Sesame: Personal Agents" from the App Store or Google Play (or use the web app at app.sesame.com where it isn't listed) and sign up
  2. Pick a character (Maya, Miles, Simone or Charlie) and call or text; the free plan gives 30 minutes a day and limited weekly credits
  3. To steer coding agents: on the Mac or Linux machine running Claude Code or Codex, run the curl install script, then sesame-link auth login and pick the default folder
  4. Add Sesame Link to the character in the Sesame app, then call and say "What are my sessions doing?" or "Focus on …"; you can also use the web client at link.sesame.com
  5. Out of allowance: upgrade to Plus or Pro or buy a top-up in the app's Plan screen, or wait for the daily or weekly reset

Watch out

Sesame hasn't published the size of the free plan's weekly credits, so how fast task use burns through them is unknown (marked unverified). The ≈$5/mo in the value line is an estimate derived from Plus at $20 for 4x the free talk time, not an official price. Sesame Link has no separate pricing page and Sesame doesn't say whether Link itself costs extra; voice control draws on Sesame's talk time and credits (marked unverified). The app isn't listed in mainland China, Hong Kong or India, where only the more limited web version works. Coding agents run on your own computer, and long plans, file contents and most MCP arguments can't be read aloud in full — confirm those in the web client or terminal. Paid plans are in-app purchases managed by the App Store or Google Play; cancel there, and don't forget after the trial.
