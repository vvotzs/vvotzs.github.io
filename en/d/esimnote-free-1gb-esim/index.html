What you get

Archived: the free eSIM cannot be installed. eSimNote's homepage pushes "Stop Paying Roaming Fees. Start with 1GB Free." and "1,000+ Free Trials Claimed Today", and claim.php offers a $0.00 "1GB Data Pack / 10 Days • 5G Speed" through three steps: pick a destination, share with one friend to unlock, scan the QR code. The 12 destinations are the US, Europe (33 countries), the UK, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Canada, Australia, Japan, Turkey and Singapore - mainland China is missing. Reading the whole page and its inline JS on 25 November 2026: "unlockShare()" only opens a WhatsApp, Facebook or Messenger share window and after two seconds unconditionally prints "Share Verified!" and unlocks the next step; "startGeneration()" runs a fake 30-second progress bar (Allocating 5G Bandwidth... then Generating Unique ID... then Finalizing...) and its "showFinal()" is commented "// Generate Dummy QR", drawing a QR image from api.qrserver.com whose payload is "LPA:1$esimnote.com$TRIAL-<timestamp>" - an invented activation string, not a carrier profile. There is no form, fetch or backend, no email is sent, and the page still carries three AD SPACE placeholders.

Archived and unobtainable. Historic terms: an eSIM-capable phone (the page lists iPhone XS-14, Galaxy S20-S23, Pixel 3-7, Huawei P40 and Mate 40); the flow asks you to share the link with one friend before the QR code unlocks; none of the 12 destinations include mainland China.

Steps

  1. Open esimnote.com and press Claim Free 1GB to reach claim.php
  2. Pick a destination (US, Europe 33, UK, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Canada, Australia, Japan, Turkey, Singapore) and press Continue
  3. Share via WhatsApp, Facebook or Messenger, wait two seconds for "Share Verified!", then press Generate QR Code

Watch out

Archived as unusable: the QR code is just a picture drawn by api.qrserver.com, encoding an invented "LPA:1$esimnote.com$TRIAL-<timestamp>" string rather than a carrier profile, so a phone cannot install it and no data follows. "Share to unlock" is not verified at all - it passes after two seconds. The page has no form, endpoint or email delivery, so do not enter personal data or pay anything here; the homepage and claim page use "1,000+ Free Trials Claimed Today" and "256-Bit SSL Secure Connection" plus ad slots to look legitimate, which is bait-page behaviour.
