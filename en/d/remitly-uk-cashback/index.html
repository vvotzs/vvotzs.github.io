What you get

Click Get cashback on a UK cashback site's Remitly page, register a UK Remitly account in the same browser session and send a first international transfer for a fixed payment. TopCashback UK pays £8 for new customers on £100+; Quidco shows £6.40 at its Premium rate (about £6 standard); KidStart pays up to £15 into a child's savings account rather than withdrawable cash; ShopBuddies pays up to £8 and Rakuten up to £7. Cashback shows as pending within 24 hours and becomes withdrawable once Remitly confirms (Quidco pays within six months). Remitly's own first-transfer offer (no fee, rate boost or discount, per the signup quote) usually applies automatically and stacks, but cashback sites generally void rewards for unapproved promo codes.

Conditions

New Remitly customers; £100 minimum first transfer (some comparison sites say £200, so follow the order page); start from the cashback link and finish in one session with ad and price-comparison blockers off; TopCashback excludes unapproved codes and Quidco excludes extra discounts that cut the sale price, so a friend's referral code can void cashback; Quidco's £6.40 needs Premium (£1 a month deducted from cashback).

Steps

  1. Register or sign in to TopCashback UK or Quidco (or check GotCashback first to see who pays most that day)
  2. Search Remitly on the cashback site and click Get cashback, with ad blockers and coupon extensions off
  3. Register a new UK Remitly account in the same browser session, complete KYC and confirm the first-transfer offer has applied
  4. Send a first transfer of £100+ to a third-party recipient (not yourself and not the inviter) and let it be delivered
  5. Check the pending record on the cashback site within 24 hours; it becomes withdrawable after the merchant confirms (Quidco up to 6 months)

Watch out

The "$25 off Remitly" links circulating online are almost all US-only codes and do nothing in the UK; cashback is confirmed after the fact, so a cancelled transfer, closed account or merchant rejection means nothing at all — don't budget it into the first transfer; KidStart's £15 is a tier cap and only goes into a child's savings account; "fee-free" usually just moves the cost into the FX spread, so compare what the recipient actually receives.
